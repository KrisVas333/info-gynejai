The 10 mistakes kids make online — and the class that fixes them

Children are online — in Roblox, Fortnite, YouTube and TikTok — years before school teaches them how information, money, and strangers actually work there. This is the problem, the evidence, and a 45-minute weekly class built to close the gap.

01 · The ProblemKids arrive online unprepared — and the platforms are designed to exploit that

Today's worry has a name. Jean Twenge framed this cohort as “the smartphone generation,” and Jonathan Haidt's The Anxious Generation (2024) turned it into the defining parent-and-teacher conversation. But anxiety isn't a skill. The real gap is that 6–8 year-olds already play and watch on platforms engineered to take their attention, data, and money — while most receive little or no guidance on how to handle it.

Two things are true at once, and both point to the same answer: kids don't need to be kept away from technology forever — they need to be taught to defend themselves in it early, the way we teach road safety before we teach driving.

02 · The EvidenceThe 10 crucial mistakes, ranked

Ranked by harm × how early it hits × how fixable it is by teaching. Each carries an evidence badge: STRONG — verified primary source or FRAMEWORK — curriculum-backed, prevalence to confirm. This honesty is deliberate — it tells us which lessons rest on hard data and which need a second research pass.

1

Oversharing personal & family information

STRONG
Why at this age

6-year-olds don't grasp “an audience” — a game chat feels like talking to one friend. They'll say name, school, address, and “I'm home alone.”

Skill that prevents it

Public vs private information; the rule “does this help a stranger find me?”

📌 CEOP (UK National Crime Agency): everyday family photos are now being misused to generate illegal AI child-abuse imagery — a live, current threat.

2

Falling for money traps & manipulation (dark patterns, loot boxes, in-app buys)

STRONG
Why at this age

Less impulse control; a “continue” button silently becomes a “buy” button; fake currency (V-Bucks, Robux) hides that real money is spent.

Skill that prevents it

Recognise “this wants my money / attention”; stop; ask a trusted adult before buying/tapping.

📌 FTC fined Epic $520M; “most children will have been caught, because they've been clicking, clicking, clicking.” UK Gov: block child loot-box purchases without a parent. Best-documented harm of all.

3

Falling for scams: “free V-Bucks/Robux,” “you won a prize,” phishing

FRAMEWORK
Why at this age

Desire for free skins/currency + no “too good to be true” instinct = clicks phishing links, gives away logins.

Skill that prevents it

“Too good to be true = a trick.” Stop → don't click → tell an adult.

📌 Framework-covered (Common Sense, Be Internet Awesome “Don't fall for fake”). Dedicated FTC/FBI child-scam prevalence data = a flagged second-pass item.

4

Can't tell ads from content, or real from fake

STRONG
Why at this age

A 6-year-old recognises only ~25% of ads on a page; “influencer says buy this” reads as a friend's advice, not a paid ad. Now compounded by AI-generated images.

Skill that prevents it

“Is this an ad? Is this real or made-up? Who made it and why?”

📌 Ali, Blades, Oates & Blumberg, 2009 (BJDP, N=401). The core information-literacy skill.

5

Trusting the wrong “friends” — stranger contact & grooming red flags

STRONG
Why at this age

“Someone I play with = my friend.” Games default chat ON with strangers. Red flags (“don't tell your parents,” move to DMs, send a pic) aren't recognised.

Skill that prevents it

People online aren't always who they say; a secret from parents is the biggest red flag; tell a trusted adult.

📌 FTC: Fortnite's on-by-default chat exposed kids to harassment & trauma → chat now off-by-default for children. CEOP grooming guidance.

6

Not telling a trusted adult / not knowing where to get help

STRONG
Why at this age

The biggest systemic gap. When something goes wrong, kids stay silent or tell a friend — rarely someone who can help.

Skill that prevents it

Name your 5 trusted adults (“Hand of 5”); it's never your fault; speaking up is brave.

📌 EU Kids Online 2020 (25,101 children): many receive no guidance and confide in parents/friends, not professionals. CEOP: calm, open conversation.

7

Cyberbullying — as target and as bystander

FRAMEWORK
Why at this age

A screen hides feelings; mean words feel “less real”; bystanders don't know they can help.

Skill that prevents it

Be kind; be an upstander (tell an adult + support the target).

📌 Common Sense “Legs/upstander” strand; FTC notes harassment on Fortnite. Bystander-behaviour prevalence = second-pass item.

8

Posting & commenting mistakes (what to post/not; how to comment/not)

FRAMEWORK
Why at this age

No sense of “permanent” or “everyone can see”; comments posted in anger or oversharing.

Skill that prevents it

Digital footprint (“it stays”); think before you post; kind comments only.

📌 Common Sense “Feet/digital footprint” + “Heart/relationships” strands.

9

Installing risky software — mods, cheats, “free” apps → malware

FRAMEWORK
Why at this age

Wants cheats/skins; downloads anything; “free” hides the cost.

Skill that prevents it

Never install without a trusted adult; “free” often isn't.

📌 Framework-based; dedicated malware-from-mods prevalence = second-pass item.

10

Weak password & account security

FRAMEWORK
Why at this age

Shares passwords with friends; uses “123456”; no idea accounts get stolen.

Skill that prevents it

A password is a secret; make it strong & fun; never share (except a parent).

📌 Be Internet Awesome “Secure your secrets”; under-10 password/2FA data = second-pass item.

05 · The SolutionBrAIn Club Jr — „Informacijos Gynėjai"

A once-weekly 45-min class where kids become Defenders of Information — catching Kaleidas's mistakes so they never make them. Story-driven, gamified, bilingual (LT + EN).

🧩 The format (deliberately minimal)

  • 45 min · 20–30 kids · 1 teacher
  • Needs only: paper, pen, 1 screen, 1 projector, internet
  • Web-based tools + tests (no installs) · physical + handwriting activities
  • Take-home artifact (badge) + 20-min parent task
